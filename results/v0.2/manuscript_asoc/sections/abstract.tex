\begin{abstract}
SOCIAL is a population-based optimiser whose agents exchange information over a synthetic small-world graph, weighted by betweenness centrality and fitness-based influence. We ask whether replacing that graph with a chemically meaningful network, the MOFGalaxyNet similarity graph of metal--organic frameworks (MOFs), helps SOCIAL select which MOFs to evaluate under a fixed budget of expensive band-gap calculations. We build MOFGalaxyNet over 8{,}697 single-linker, single-metal MOFs from QMOF and port SOCIAL's update equations to finite-pool acquisition through an embedding and a snap-to-nearest-unevaluated operator (Graph-SOCIAL). We compare it with nine baselines on four band-gap objectives, four budgets (0.5--5\% of the pool) and 30 seeds, inside a pipeline with pre-specified stop/go gates. Band gaps are strongly homophilous on the graph (neighbour correlation $r=0.52$ against 0.00 for degree-preserving nulls). The central hypothesis was nevertheless not supported: in the pre-specified pilot, MOFGalaxyNet did not beat a degree-preserving random topology ($p=0.20$). With 30 seeds, its advantage was small and objective-dependent (Cliff's $\delta\leq0.22$, achieved power $\leq0.64$, not significant after correction), and a Watts--Strogatz graph did better on one objective. High-betweenness neighbours did not lead to new chemical families more often than expected. Graph-SOCIAL outperformed random search only on the minimum-gap objective, and that advantage disappeared when its chemistry-aware embedding was replaced by geometric descriptors. Bootstrap random-forest Thompson sampling and snap-based PSO, DE and GA ranked higher overall. An audit of MOFGalaxyNet further shows that its published edge count corresponds to a similarity threshold near 0.7 rather than the stated 0.9. All code, data versions, gates and deviations are released.
\end{abstract}
